The Student interval assumes independent identically normal readings, unknown
population mean and unknown positive spread. The entered sample standard
deviation uses n−1 in its definition. Changing whole n holds both observed
summaries fixed to isolate the two effects on width: standard error and the
Student multiplier. Numerical 0.975 quantiles for degrees of freedom 1–30
are constants generated by angle-transformed Simpson quadrature, checked
independently using the analytic cosine-integral recurrence in Python.
Closed forms for one and two degrees of freedom provide additional checks.
The interval has repeated-procedure 95% coverage under the model; it is not
a prediction interval or a posterior probability for the unknown mean.
Unknown mu has no invented numerical position.

The randomization reference enumerates every one of the 70 uniformly possible
four-versus-four allocations of eight learners. A sharp no-effect null keeps
the same eight scores fixed for every allocation. The two-sided convention
counts absolute mean differences at least as large as observed, including ties.
For these seven fixed integer scores and one adjustable score, the comparisons
reduce algebraically to integer thresholds for the adjustable score. They do
not use a floating tie tolerance; tests include neighboring representable
values at thresholds. An independent Python oracle enumerates exact fractions.
The displayed allocation is a selected construction, not a generated draw.
Learners use ordinary numbers 1–8; zero-based indices stay inside enumeration.

One-way ANOVA reconstructs three groups of three packing times. The runtime's
closed forms for between/within sums of squares agree with independently
reconstructed observations, group means and grand mean in Python. Dividing
by degrees of freedom 2 and 6 produces the mean squares and F ratio. The
F(2,6) upper-tail formula `(1+F/3)^−3` assumes independent normal errors,
common positive variance and equal population means under the null. The
constructed times do not diagnose those assumptions, establish causation or
identify which pairs of means differ.
