# Geometric series and partial sums

Draft teaching specimen. A registered interactive renderer is available in the accompanying application; retained source fields describe the original specimen and its pending review.

Definition unit math:concept:geometric-series; revision 0.1.0-draft. Setting: A positioning script sends a first forward correction a centimetres. Each later signed correction is r times the previous correction. Add exactly n commands to find the endpoint. A negative ratio alternates forward and backward corrections; the signed sum is net displacement, not distance travelled or a simulation of a mechanical controller. Expression: $S_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}ar^k=\begin{cases}a\frac{1-r^n}{1-r},&r\ne1,\\na,&r=1.\end{cases}\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}ar^k=\frac{a}{1-r}\ \ (|r|<1).$. Conditions required for a defined value (a case may test their violation): The first correction a lies in [0.25,4] cm, ratio r in [−1.5,1.5], and n is a whole number from 1 through 12. Every finite sum is defined, including r=1. Because a is nonzero, the infinite series has a finite sum only for |r|<1. Integer commands do not interpolate into fractional terms. Numeric sums use a compensated recurrence; nonzero term underflow is reported explicitly. ## Purpose

Where does the stage finish after a whole number of repeated corrections?

## Meaning

A geometric series adds terms with one common ratio. A partial sum adds a finite whole number of terms. Its closed form has a separate r=1 case. With a nonzero first term, the infinite geometric series has a finite sum exactly when the ratio has magnitude less than one.

## Properties

- Item:
  - claim: Finite partial sums remain defined when no infinite-series sum exists.
  - conditions: The first correction a lies in [0.25,4] cm, ratio r in [−1.5,1.5], and n is a whole number from 1 through 12. Every finite sum is defined, including r=1. Because a is nonzero, the infinite series has a finite sum only for |r|<1. Integer commands do not interpolate into fractional terms. Numeric sums use a compensated recurrence; nonzero term underflow is reported explicitly.
  - derivation: Subtract r times a finite sum from that sum to obtain (1−r)Sₙ=a(1−rⁿ). When r=1, add n equal terms. For |r|<1 the power tends to zero; for |r|≥1 with nonzero a the terms do not tend to zero.
